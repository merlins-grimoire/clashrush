## Closed model

`QueueState` is exactly `WAITING | ELIGIBLE | DEFERRED | QUARANTINED | ADMITTED`. `Lane` is exactly `HOME | BUILDER`. `ALLOWED_TRANSITIONS` is the complete transition graph, exposed through an immutable mapping whose values are frozen sets.

Every `QueueJob` binds:

- one stable `AccountKey` and immutable `ConfigurationGeneration`;
- a positive `JobGeneration`;
- the original 32-lowercase-hex `AvailabilityEvent` identity;
- one finite `available_at` and one persisted finite `due_at` that cannot precede it;
- the pending Home or Builder lane;
- only state-appropriate reconciliation, yield, or quarantine metadata, with an exact prior state and no self-account or repeated-account yield tokens.

Admission requires a currently due `ELIGIBLE` job and creates one immutable `VisitAdmission`: account/configuration/job generation, a positive visit generation, and the caller-injected exact 32-lowercase-hex visit nonce. An `AdmittedVisit` accepts only exact `QueueJob` and `VisitAdmission` values, requires the job to be `ADMITTED`, and requires exact account/configuration/job-generation agreement, including on direct construction. The nonce is generated by the future durable queue transaction and must be passed unchanged to schema-v2 `LifecycleSupervisor.start_admitted`; this module never creates a competing nonce. Sealed v1 `LifecycleSupervisor.start` is not imported, called, or modified.

Completion consumes only an exact, cross-field-validated `AdmittedVisit`, never a directly constructed unbound `ADMITTED` job, into one fresh higher job generation. The successor carries its own original availability event and exactly one deadline. A future builder event's randomized offset is chosen and persisted by the future storage/orchestration layer before constructing the job; this contract never redraws it. A positively free builder uses visit finish as both availability and due time. Unknown state uses bounded deferral.

## Ordering, deferral, and fairness

Selection requires every tuple member to be an exact `QueueJob`, every account-order member and non-null tie cursor to be an exact `AccountKey`, considers only currently due `ELIGIBLE` jobs, and fails closed if more than one candidate belongs to the same account, even when their job generations or deadlines differ. It then selects by oldest persisted deadline. Only jobs sharing that oldest deadline use `tie_after` plus injected stable configuration order, making equal-deadline order restart-stable. Future, deferred, quarantined, and admitted jobs are ineligible.

A deferral preserves account, configuration, job generation, original availability event, deadline, and pending lane. It accepts only exact `QueueJob` and `SchedulerLimits` values, and its injected `reconcile_at` must be inclusively 5–60 minutes after `now` and later than the original due deadline. Its immutable `yield_set` snapshots at most one exact competing `(account_key, job_generation)` per other account. Re-entry requires both the persisted time and an empty caller-resolved barrier; a persisted reconciliation deadline may already be overdue when loaded. The persistence layer may resolve a token only after that exact generation receives terminal admission or independently becomes waiting/quarantined. Eligibility, quarantine, release, admission, and completion helpers likewise reject subclasses and attribute-compatible fakes at their owned ingress. Quarantine preserves its exact restorable prior state and all deadline/lane metadata.

## Frozen execution bounds

`SchedulerLimits` freezes a 600-second visit, initial 300-second Home opportunity and 300-second Builder opportunity, inclusive 300–3600-second reconciliation, exactly 1000 total iterations, and exactly 3 consecutive failures; constructor overrides cannot narrow or widen these constants. These are admission-loop ceilings, not permission to overrun a safe cleanup.

`scheduler_budget.py` is the pure outer-loop enforcement seam. One immutable, mutation-checked `RunBudget` carries its original start, caller-owned global deadline, latest monotonic check, total iterations, and consecutive failures through every `CONTINUE` and `SWAP`. `run_bounded_rotations` validates and detaches its ingress state before invoking the clock, checks all three bounds before every callback that may select or set up work, gives that callback a detached copy of the current global counters, records one exact success/failure result against its private authoritative state, resets only the consecutive-failure counter on success, and gives a reached safety bound precedence over a simultaneous requested stop. It rejects regressing or non-finite clocks and malformed or post-construction-mutated ingress/callback values. Private fixed numeric ceilings prevent mutation of the shared `DEFAULT_LIMITS` object from widening run or visit authority. The contract deliberately does not invent a fixed fleet-run duration: its deadline is injected once at run creation and can never be replaced by a rotation result or callback mutation.

Each immutable, mutation-checked `VisitBudget` freezes its reservation boundary at start plus 300 seconds and its hard deadline at start plus 600 seconds. Before the boundary, the configured first/pending lane retains priority while runnable. At and after the boundary, every not-yet-observed runnable lane overrides caller preference until both Home and Builder have received a real opportunity; only then may the caller transfer unused time by preference. An unavailable first lane does not block the other, and a first lane that becomes runnable later remains protected rather than being starved by the already-observed peer. Post-construction mutation of deadlines or observed-lane evidence is rejected before selection, and no lane is returned at or after the fixed deadline.

## Isolation and downstream contract

This model and budget driver are pure and injected: no filesystem, SQLite, lifecycle, Win32/device, process, capture/input, spending, network/Discord, sleep, environment, or private/runtime-path access. They neither activate nor reconcile lifecycle state. The scheduler persistence seam owns admission and action journaling; later orchestration under the lifecycle mutex proves process absence before cursor/successor finalization. Existing production callers remain untouched.

The matching tests are `tests/test_scheduler_contract.py`: immutable legal graph and admission-bound completion; illegal/stale transitions; exact helper and nested-value ingresses; malformed or mismatched direct admission/deferred/quarantine construction; non-finite and generation/nonce boundaries; one-deadline preservation; exact `QueueJob` selection ingress, duplicate-account rejection, oldest-due ordering, and exact restart-stable tie cursors; exact `SchedulerLimits` deferral ingress and 5/60-minute reconciliation boundaries; yield-set fairness; quarantine restoration; and exact lane, visit, and run limits. `tests/test_scheduler_budget.py` adds hard 5/10-minute visit boundaries, protected lane selection including delayed-runnable starvation prevention, repeated-swap iteration and failure ceilings, success-only failure reset, actual global time accounting, monotonic-clock rejection, pre-callback deadline enforcement, callback/clock alias mutation isolation, post-construction budget mutation rejection, and shared-limits mutation resistance.

## Non-replayable action journal

Schema v3 adds one durable action journal to the same WAL/FULL SQLite database as the admitted job and visit. Every action binds a positive global action generation, a store-generated 32-lowercase-hex action nonce, the exact admitted visit generation/nonce, its Home or Builder lane, and a caller-supplied 64-lowercase-hex fingerprint of the complete immutable intent. A store-derived SHA-256 binding seal covers that identity plus the current state and sticky input markers, detecting any coherent downgrade back to a replayable state as well as changed generation, nonce, fingerprint, visit binding, or lane on reopen. The fingerprint is globally unique and retained after terminal completion: retrying the same intent under a fresh action nonce therefore fails closed instead of replaying it. The caller must derive the fingerprint canonically from the complete intent plus account/configuration/job identity; the store accepts no raw intent payload or private identifier.

The only progression is `PLANNED → INTENT_RECORDED → INPUT_STARTED → INPUT_COMPLETED → CONFIRMED|FAILED|UNCERTAIN`, with reconciliation also permitted to close `INPUT_STARTED` directly to a terminal outcome. Journal actions use validated tuple-backed storage rather than field-mutable dataclasses, and every transition rebuilds a service-owned canonical value before use. One schema-enforced nonterminal singleton prevents a second action from being planned while any input boundary remains unresolved. `load_replayable_actions` exposes only `PLANNED` and `INTENT_RECORDED`; durable `INPUT_STARTED`, `INPUT_COMPLETED`, and every terminal outcome are excluded after reopen. Exact expected-record comparisons make stale or duplicate transition calls fail without mutation.

Terminalization runs under one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction: it updates the action to one exact terminal outcome, clears the nonterminal singleton, and updates the admitted job plus its immutable visit snapshot to the exact next pending lane. Any failed write rolls all three changes back. Reopen validates canonical schema-v3 tables, admission equality, every journal value, terminal/nonterminal marker consistency, and every nonterminal action's binding to the one open visit. Canonical v1/v2 databases migrate forward only after their earlier admission invariants pass; no action is synthesized during migration. This slice performs no lifecycle, process, device, capture/input, spending, network, Discord, private-state, or successor action.

`tests/test_scheduler_journal.py` proves restart persistence at `INPUT_STARTED` and `INPUT_COMPLETED`, replay filtering, duplicate transition rejection, globally stable intent fingerprints after `UNCERTAIN`, one nonterminal action across restart, atomic terminal-outcome/pending-lane commit and rollback, journal-integrity rejection, and canonical v2-to-v3 migration. Scheduler admission/store tests additionally cover v1-to-v3 migration and preserve the earlier atomic-admission invariants.
